Narrator:
Part of my inaugural duties was to take a tour of the Empire.
Naturally, the territorial extent of that Empire changed drastically from our last coronation, and thus it was decided rather than simply visiting various regions on Isa Bellmaré, I’d journey through only the most vital provinces, then take my voyage to the capitals of our new subjects and colonies.
Several Dominions, a junior Empire, Federation, and Principality later, I arrived at the Kingdom of Aeteran, once vassal to the Kammarian Empire, now both fly the Suzerish banner.
Although a Kingdom, it never officially had a King since Kammaria first concocted the puppet state after the second bout in the 40 Years War.
A Prince-Regent, named Józiff Ivanothy, was proclaimed whilst the future King was to be decided on. That evidently has not yet come to pass.
I was led by enthusiastic Aeteran Nobles through the royal palace, Almörthy. They showed me various turrets and towers which protruded from the main structure, great halls lined with tapestries of various hunts and battles the Nobles took part in, all interiorly designed in dark carved wood and arches that gave a serious character to the whole place.
The wooden and rug-laden floors made an unusual sound I wasn’t used to, nothing like the slick marble we used in Suzerain.
Eventually I was brought to the main dining hall housing a massive decorated tile stove, a staple of Aeteran. It was made of lime green and rose pink ceramic, the only source of color in an otherwise dark chamber.
Its warm radiance was a Godsend for the cold region. They had just entered the peak of winter season, while back home we were well into spring.
Being already at the end of my wits for small talk, feasts, dancing, speeches, keeping up appearances, and so much more, I was perhaps a bit more curt than meaning to be. However, I found the Aeterans almost enjoyed the straightforward and rough nature I spoke in. Maybe to them these formal walls were a chore they only put up with us Suzerains, and they liked seeing me behave more like them, even if accidentally.
Freshly killed meats were laid at the table along with dark malt beer, very different from the lavish wine I’d been downing all week.
Taking some time to eat and drink, the Nobles eventually started their banter, even singing and shouting to one another merrily. One could’ve mistaken this gathering for a Commoner’s tavern, not the Emperor’s honorary banquet.
The Prince-Regent, sat opposite me, was digging into a fat-ridden slab of meat and happily nodding along to the loud ramblings of a Noble shouting in his ear. I could barely catch the conversation over all the other noise, but I think it was about the current tax on hops.
Just then, a Noble sitting across the table to my left, bearing a large bushy beard dripping with freshly sipped beer, bowed his head and spoke.

Zichy Hoovitz:
“Your Grace-! Er- Your MAJESTY, I mean! The name’s Duke Zichy Hoovitz of Straud. Might I say, it’s such a breath of fresh air to have a sovereign who can let loose on typical decorum when opportunity permits!”
“Your Grandmother, with all due respect, was a bit of a sour fruit when she visited us. Never indulged in drink nor mischief. We always called her the ‘Iron Nun’, if you get the meaning!”
“Your young blood is just what we need to get some life back in the Empire! Again, no offense, but I doubt your aging Father would’ve been fit for the job either.”
