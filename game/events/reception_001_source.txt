Narrator:
Cheers of elation, showers of flower pedals, and the thunder of marching soldiers. This is what engulfed my senses as I was driven by carriage to the royal palace.
It truly was beyond the scale of even my Grandmother’s coronation, which despite having been only a child at the time, I remembered especially among a flood of other imperial celebrations.
Being the heir to a now star spanning empire—in fact the largest in known galactic history—carried with it some necessity to eclipse any previous levels of grandeur. I knew my government had a field day concocting all manner of ways to spend our ever growing treasury in this regard. 
My favorite thus far was the proposal to terraform our largest moon to resemble my visage, however nonsensical it was.
Riding down the cobblestone streets of Isa Bellmaré, with Suzerain’s royal heraldry hung over every conceivable space, I thought more of my Grandmother. How I used to sit beside her and my parents in this very carriage, watching her wave at our subjects in the graceful manner befitting an Empress. 
She seemed so perfect in those moments, so absolute, so beyond comparison. 
Yet just before I’d begin to think my Grandmother couldn’t possibly pay any mind to someone like me, someone mortal, she’d give a glance and warm smile, as if to say “My dear, none of this is more important than you”. 
That’s the version of Sicill held fondly in my mind. Beside this aura of unassailability which most of Suzerain viewed her with, I recalled when she’d help lift me on the palace balcony to wave down at her gathered subjects, how we’d quietly throw crumbs of bread at one another during dinner with impish smiles.
How we mourned my Father’s passing.
She’d tell me my Father would be so proud of the man I was shaping up to be, that I’d soon become the greatest Emperor in all of Suzerain’s history.
I always hoped she was right. Yet I couldn't shake the thought of Sicill's shadow—how I'd spend my entire reign cast beneath it, measured against her. 
I shook this concern out of mind. Today was one of new beginnings, of my turn to shape Suzerain, and now the galaxy.
Making our final journey towards Catheljunta, the imperial palace and my home, a swarm of commoners tried to follow in pace, becoming quickly fatigued at the steep mountainous climb, but pushing through their weariness all the same. I saw the boiling and panting faces of many Suzerains as they jogged, parents even carrying their children, all in an effort to simply be as close to me as possible throughout this last stretch of public viewing.
Beside my carriage ran a rather young boy, whose friends were leagues behind him, puffing his cheeks and exerting every effort to not fall behind me. He snuck glances in my direction every chance he got, though to avoid losing his focus and being overtaken by the surrounding crowd, he kept turning his head violently from side to front. 
His rigid arms, serious expression, and stiff motions made him to me appear a soldier.

I nodded my farewell to them.
After many, many, many more visitors, I finally was permitted to stand from the throne and get myself some food and drink.
If I had to have sat in that throne any longer, I would've lost a part of my sanity.
Estates, foreign rulers, my family, keeping track of them I knew would prove a feat in its own right, let alone pleasing them all.
Though perhaps it would be better to consolidate my efforts on a select few? It would draw the ire of those left out of my favor, indeed. Yet I am sovereign, such choices are mine to make.
Or does even my absolute rule require the consent of others? This I pondered on.
Scouring whatever was left at the banquet tables, I obtained a strong glass of wine, and tried to compile a meal of my favorite meats and pairings.
All of it had long grown cold, but it was better than nothing.
